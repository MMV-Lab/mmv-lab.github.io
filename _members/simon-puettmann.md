Simon is a PhD Researcher at the Leibniz-Institut für Analytische Wissenschaften – ISAS – e.V. in the AMBIOM group, where he develops scalable AI-based algorithms for biomedical image analysis, focusing on large-scale 3D microscopy data. His research aims to enable data-driven studies of disease mechanisms at the level of entire organs and organ systems, as well as to support clinical decision-making.

His current work centers on large-scale light-sheet microscopy of whole, optically cleared organs. This includes the quantification of disease-affected tissue (e.g., post-stroke brain) and the segmentation and analysis of vascular networks across a range of tissue types (from native mouse organs to 3D-printed vascular networks engineered within hydrogel-based tissue scaffolds). By spanning neurological, musculoskeletal, and tissue-engineering applications, his research focuses on image-analysis methods designed to generalize across diverse biological questions rather than remaining tailored to single use cases.

Previously, Simon completed a Master’s degree in Computer Science at the University of Applied Sciences and Arts Dortmund, focusing on deep learning methods for medical image analysis. During his studies, he conducted research in Switzerland within the MedGIFT group as part of the EU-funded ExaMode project, culminating in a Master’s thesis on predicting the time to biochemical recurrence in prostate cancer patients.
